Here is how successful models differentiate between a "True Breakout" and a "Mean Reversion Trap."

1. The "Mean-Proximity" Logic
In a classic volatility expansion setup (like the Bollinger Band Squeeze), the bands contract toward the mean.

The Goal: You want a "coiled spring." For a spring to coil, the price must consolidate. Consolidation almost always occurs near the moving average (the "equilibrium" price).

The Risk of X Std Devs: If price is already 2 or 3 standard deviations away from the 100-period average, the "rubber band" is already stretched. Even if volatility increases, the most likely immediate move is a snap-back to the mean.

The "Squeeze" Setup
Low Volatility: The width of the Bollinger Bands (usually 2 Std Devs) reaches a multi-month low.

Price Location: Price is oscillating tightly around the 20 or 100-period midline.

The Trigger: Price closes outside the narrow bands as volatility begins to tick upward.
